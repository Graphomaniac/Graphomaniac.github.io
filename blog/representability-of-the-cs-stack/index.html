    1 What do we want?

    In this post, I want to sketch a proof of the following fact:

    Theorem 1.1. Let M be a closed oriented 3-manifold with a principal G-bundle P →M, where G is compact and has an Ad ⁡ -invariant positive definite symmetric form on 𝔤. Take the derived enhancement of the zero locus of the Chern–Simons functional on the space of connections. Then the quotient stack of the derived zero locus by the group of trivial gauges of connections is a 1-geometric derived differentiable stack with the natural shifted symplectic structure.

    I will formulate a more precise version of this theorem below once all the relevant notation has been introduced.

    All the ingredients for the proof are essentially already in the literature; i.e., the combination of the work by John Pardon and Pelle Steffens [Par26]; [Ste24] provides the necessary tools to prove this representability result. However, it seems this hasn’t been written down anywhere, and it makes for a nice showcase of the theory, so I decided to write it as a blog post.

    2 Chern–Simons setup

    Consider a closed oriented 3-manifold M and consider a principal G-bundle P →M over M where G is a compact Lie group with an Ad ⁡ -invariant positive definite symmetric form on 𝔤 denoted by ⟨−,−⟩. We define the following objects:

    1)
    Conn ⁡ P (M) is the infinite-dimensional stack of connections on P over M
    2)
    Gauge ⁡ P (M) is the infinite-dimensional group stack of gauge transformations of P over M.

    Now we construct the curvature/Chern–Simons map in a more functorial way, allowing us to define the Chern–Simons derived stack as a derived fibre product.

    Consider the following pairing:

    Ω2(M;ad ⁡ (P)) ⊗Ω1(M;ad ⁡ (P)) → ℝ,(β,α)↦∫ M⟨β ∧α⟩.

    We have the right action of gauge transformations on connections/1-forms and 2-forms:

    B↦RgB,RgB = g−1𝐵𝑔 + g−1𝑑𝑔,R gβ = g−1𝛽𝑔

    Thus we can define the following map:

    Conn ⁡ P (M)×Ω2(M;ad ⁡ (P)) → Conn ⁡ P (M)×Ω3(M;ad ⁡ (P)),(B,β)↦(B,−d Bβ).

    We also have the map Conn ⁡ P (M) → Conn ⁡ P (M) ×Ω3(M;ad ⁡ (P)) that sends a connection B to the pair (B,0). We thus define the following explicit model for the restricted variational cotangent complex of [Conn ⁡ P (M)∕Gauge ⁡ P (M)]:

    𝕋var∨([Conn ⁡ P (M)∕Gauge ⁡ P (M)]) = [(Conn ⁡ P (M) ×Ω2(M;ad ⁡ (P)) × Conn ⁡ P(M)×Ω3(M;ad ⁡ (P)) Conn ⁡ P (M))/Gauge ⁡ P (M)].

    Definition 2.1. Define the derived Chern–Simons stack to be the following derived fibre product with respect to the zero and curvature maps that we have defined above:

    𝔐P CS(M) = [Conn ⁡ P (M)∕Gauge ⁡ P (M)]×𝕋var∨(X) [Conn ⁡ P (M)∕Gauge ⁡ P (M)]

    We can now formulate a more precise version of the main representability theorem.

    Theorem 2.2. The stack 𝔐P CS(M) satisfies the following properties:

    1)
    It is a locally finitely presented 1-geometric derived 𝒞∞-stack with a representable smooth atlas and a proper diagonal. In particular, it is a derived Lie orbifold.
    2)
    Its classical truncation is presented by the standard groupoid of flat connections and gauge transformations. At a flat connection A we have the following equivalence of tangent complexes:
    𝕋A𝔐P CS ≃ [Ω A0 →d AΩA1 →d AΩA2 →d AΩA3]

    Here dA is the twisted de Rham differential determined by the connection A.

    3)
    Denote by KA = Stab ⁡ 𝒢(A) the stabilizer substack of A in the group stack of gauge transformations. Then there is a KA-invariant neighbourhood UA ⊆H1(M;ad ⁡ (P)) and a KA-equivariant smooth function fA : UA → ℝ such that locally around A we have the following equivariant Darboux equivalence:
    𝔐P CS ≃ Crit ⁡ [U A∕KA] (fA).
    4)
    The equivalence in the preceeding part is compatible with the following explicit choice of a −1-shifted symplectic form:
    ΩAi ⊗Ω A3−i → ℝ,(α,β)↦∫ M⟨α ∧β⟩.

    3 Local charts for the full connections stack

    We fix a choice of a Riemannian structure on M and a particular (reference) flat connection A. In this section, we will do most of the hard analysis required to establish representability. The basic idea is quite straightforward: we want to use Banach inverse function theorems together with elliptic regularity to prove the existence of local charts. From now on, we will fix a choice of the Sobolev scale s0 ≥ 3 it is dictated by the continuity estimate given by the Sobolev embedding theorem on 3-dimensional manifold.

    Define the following three objects:

    V A(r) := ker⁡(d A∗ : HrΩ A1 →Hr−1Ω A0),𝔩 = ker⁡(d A : H0Ω A0 →H0Ω A1),ℓ(r) = 𝔩⊥∩HrΩ A0.

    The following result describes a local finite-dimensional model around the flat connection A. I call it the “Coulomb slice” because it arises from the equation similar to the classical Coulomb equation ∇ ⁡⋅a = 0, in fact this analogy could be made precise for G = U(1) on the flat space, where the codifferential dA∗ precisely matches up with the gradient ∇ ⁡.

    Lemma 3.1 (local Coulomb slice model). For a sufficiently small neigbourhood of A there is an equivalence of derived differentiable stacks of the following form:

    [(A + {Hr ker⁡d A∗} ∥−∥s<𝜀)∕KA] ≃ [Hs0 Conn ⁡ P (M)∕Hs0+1 Gauge ⁡ P (M)]

    Here the set {Hr ker⁡dA∗}∥−∥s<𝜀 is 𝜀-ball in the Sobolev norm inside of the r-regular kernel of dA∗.

    Note that this is not yet a description of the Chern–Simons stack, but rather of the ambient stack of all connections with no condition on the curvature.

    Proof

    Proof. For u ∈ℓ(s0+1) sufficiently close to the origin we apply the parametrized Banach implicit function theorem to the parametrized map of Banach spaces:

    Θ(u,a) = dA∗(R eu(A + a) −A).

    Since its derivative at u = 0 is given by

    ∂ ∂𝑢|u=0Θ(u,a) = ∂ ∂𝑢|u=0dA∗(R eu(A + a) −A)=⏟u=𝑡𝑣 = d 𝑑𝑡|t=0 [(1 + 𝑡𝑣 + O(t2))−1(A + a)(1 + 𝑡𝑣 + O(t2))(1 + 𝑡𝑣 + O(t2))−1de𝑣𝑡] = [A + a,v] + 𝑑𝑣 = d A+av.

    Thus we can evaluate at u = 0 and we get that the Fréchet derivative at u = 0 is equal to dA∗dA. This is an isomorphism of Banach spaces ℓs0+1 →ℓs0−1. Consequently, the parametric Banach implicit function theorem applies. This gives us the local model on the left.

    Now, using elliptic estimates, we will prove that the action of 𝒢s0+1 is proper near A thus this will show that we can restrict the stabilizer purely to the local stabilizer of A. Suppose that the action of Hs0+1 Gauge ⁡ P (M) is non-proper, then we can find two sequences {Bn} and {Cn} of elements in the local model A + {Hr ker⁡dA∗}∥−∥s<𝜀 such that they both converge to the reference connection and there is a sequence of gauge transformations gn conjugating one sequence to another and staying away from the stabilizer of A. The equation RgnBn = Cn is equivalent to the equation:

    dgn + Bngn −gnCn = 0.

    Since connections Bn and Cn are sufficiently close to A, we can present them using convergent sequences bn,cn where Bn = A + bn,Cn = A + cn. Define the operator1

    dAEnd : Hk+1End(E) →HkT∗M ⊗End(E),d AEndg n = gncn −bngn.

    The composite (dAEnd)∗dA is elliptic and the standard elliptic estimates gives:

    ∥gn∥Hk+1 ≤Ck(∥gncn −bngn∥Hk + ∥gn∥L2).

    Using continuity of multiplication, we get a further estimate for the right-hand side:

    ∥gncn −bngn∥Hk ≤C(∥bn∥Hs0 + ∥cn∥Hs0)∥gn∥Hk.

    Iterating this estimate (until we reduce s0 to 0) we get sup ⁡ n∥gn∥Hs0+1 < ∞. Finally, using the Rellich–Kondrashov compactness theorem, we get that there is a convergent subsequence of gn’s in Hs0. Now we use an elliptic estimate one more time, to get convergence of that subsequence in Hs0+1. It now remains to recall that because the sequence of gn’s is convergent and the limit gauge fixes A it means that gn can not stay away from KA and thus we get a contradiction. This proves the local representation of the stack asserted in the lemma. □

    Remark 3.2. In the language of Lie groupoids, we prove Morita equivalence between the groupoids locally presenting the two stacks by proving that (A + {Hr ker⁡dA∗}∥−∥s<𝜀) gives a slice for the action groupoid on the right.

    4 Local form of the Chern–Simons stack via Hodge theory

    Now, the goal is to prove that the Chern–Simons stack admits nice local charts that could be modelled by equivariant derived critical loci with their natural (−1)-shifted symplectic structures. To this end, I want to bootstrap the proof of the finite-dimensionality of the Chern–Simons stack from another famously finite-dimensional representability problem, namely that for harmonic representatives of cohomology classes on a Riemannian manifold. The idea is that, for the Chern–Simons stack, we can repeat this story locally to obtain the requisite charts.

    Before diving into the construction, I want to describe an infinitesimal approximation of this story that yields the formal version of the same representability statement. This is fairly standard; our main task would then be to establish "analytic convergence" of the constructions we are making.

    4.1 An analogy with formal geometry

    Take the dg Lie algebra LA = (Ω∙(M;ad ⁡ (P)),dA,[−,−]) using the standard Hodge-theoretic contraction of LA onto its harmonic representatives, we get an L∞ (higher Lie) algebra HA∙ = H∙(M;ad ⁡ (P)) = ker⁡ ⁡ dA ∩ker⁡ ⁡ dA∗. We have the standard homological perturbation lemma setup:

    pA : ΩA∙→H A∙,i A : HA∙→Ω∙,h A : ΩAi →Ω Ai−1.

    Here pA is the harmonic projector, iA is the inclusion of harmonic forms, and hA is the contracting homotopy on the antiharmonic forms. The last fact is also expressed by the following chain homotopy identity:

    dAhA + hAdA = id −iApA.

    Now, we want to transfer the Chern–Simons/Maurer–Cartan equation along the contraction onto the harmonic cohomology. However, there is one small problem: if we just take the harmonic projection it will not preserve the equation because its non-linear:

    FA+a = dAa + 1 2[a,a] = 0

    So if x is the harmonic component of a satisfying FA+a = 0 it’s not necessarily true that [x,x] = 0 and so the projected equation need not be true. So instead of just projecting a to its harmonic component, we can introduce an additional component w(x) that represents the non-harmonic correction to a. As a result, we get the following second equation in the anti-harmonic direction:

    Πim ⁡ dA∗ (Πker⁡dA∗ {∗(dAa + 1 2[a ∧a])}) = 0

    The expression the first projection applies to is the L2-gradient of the Chern–Simons equation restricted to the Coulomb slice.

    Then if we treat w formally, we can write down the formula for w(x) using the above anti-harmonic part of the equation:

    w(x) = −1 2hA [x + w(x),x + w(x)]

    then homological perturbation gives us a formal expansion for w(x):

    w(x) = −1 2hA[x,x] + higher terms

    Finally, we substitute the formula for w(x) into the harmonic part of the equation:

    pA(dA(x + w(x)) + 1 2[x + w(x),x + w(x)]) = 0.

    Now, define the map fA(x) = CSA(x + w(x)) it is then immediately apparent that the above equation is equivalent to the equation dfA(x)[h] = ∫ M⟨h ∧FA+x+w(x)⟩ = 0. In terms of the L∞-structure transferred from the twisted de Rham complex, one can express dfA(x) combinatorially:

    dfA(x) = ∑ n≥2 1 n!ℓn(x,…,x)∗.

    Here (−)∗ denotes Poincaré duality.

    4.2 The actual construction

    Now, after the above formal (in the mathematical sense) sketch, I will explain in gory detail why everything done above actually holds non-perturbatively.

    We have the following Sobolev version of the Hodge decomposition for HrΩA1:

    HrΩ A1 = Hr ker⁡(d A∗) ⊕d Aℓ(r+1).

    Consider a connection B = A + a with a ∈Hr ker⁡dA∗, at B the gauge directions are represented by dBℓ(r+1). We define the operator that parametrizes this decomposition:

    ιa : Hr ker⁡(d A∗) ⊕ℓ(r+1) →(v,ξ)↦v + d BξHrΩ1 ra : Hr−1Ω A2 →β↦Π ker⁡dA∗(∗β) ⊕ΠℓdB∗(∗β)Hr−1 ker⁡(d A∗) ⊕ℓr−2

    Now, we can use these two operators to get the “normal form” of the Maurer–Cartan equation. We start from the twisted de Rham complex that has an additive splitting by Hodge theory (we omit the Sobolev parameter for now):

    𝔩 ⊕ℓ⏟Ω A0=“gauge parameters” →ξ↦dA+aξker⁡ ⁡(dA∗) ⊕ℓ⏟ ΩA1=“fields” →F(−) ker⁡ ⁡(dA∗) ⊕ℓ⏟ ΩA2=“equations” →d−F−𝔩∗⊕ℓ⏟ ΩA3=“identities” (1)

    Note that the middle map A + a↦FA+a is non-linear. Maps ιa and ra, gives us the following identities:

    ιa−1d A+a = ( [a,−] 0 0 idℓ ) ,raFA+a = Πker⁡dA∗(∗FA+a)⊕0

    Thus (1) projects to the following chain of maps:

    𝔩 →[a,−]Hr ker⁡d A∗→Π ker⁡dA∗(∗FA+a)Hr−1 ker⁡d A∗→[a,−]∨𝔩∗

    The map in the middle is the L2-gradient on the Coulomb slice of the Chern–Simons action functional:

    CSA(a) = 1 2∫ M⟨a ∧dAa⟩ + 1 6∫ M⟨a ∧ [a,a]⟩.

    Now, we can use the implicit function theorem to prove that there exists a unique map wA : UA ⊆HA1 →H∞im ⁡ dA∗ solving the equation:

    Πim ⁡ dA∗Πker⁡dA∗(∗FA+x+wA(x)) = 0.

    The implicit function theorem is applicable because the differential of Πker⁡dA∗(∗FA+x) at the origin is ∗dA which is an isomorphism from Hr im ⁡ dA∗→Hr−1 im ⁡ dA∗.

    The following lemma summarizes the properties of the Hodge-theoretic splitting we have built.

    Lemma 4.1. After possibly shrinking open neighbourhoods we get a KA-equivariant local isomorphism

    Ξ : H∞ker⁡d A∗→H A1 ×H∞im ⁡ d A∗,x + w↦(x,Π im ⁡ dA∗∗Πker⁡ ⁡ dA∗(x + w))

    The proof is another instance of applying elliptic bootstrapping, and I’ll skip it for now. I might add it to the post later if there is interest.

    Now, if we consider ∇ ⁡L2CS(Ξ−1(x,y)) = (φ(x,y),y). I claim that the following identity holds:

    φ(x,0) = dfA(x),under the equivalence HA1 ≃ (H A1)∗

    Here the function fA : UA → ℝ is given, as before, by

    fA(x) = CS(A + x + wA(x)) −CS(A).

    Observe, that the dependence of the connection shift a on x is given by ax = Ξ−1(x,0) = x + wA(x). Thus we have:

    dfA(x)[v] = ⟨∇ ⁡L2CS(ax),DxΞ−1(v)⟩ L2 = ⟨∇ ⁡L2CS(ax),v+DwA(x)[v]⟩ = ⟨φ(x,0),v⟩.

    In the last identity, we use the fact that DwA(x)[v] is perpendicular to HA1. On the slice, y = 0 we get the residual stabilizer, or Noether, action given by [ax,−] and [ax,−]∨. We are now ready to define the local charts for the Chern–Simons stack using the slice construction above.

    Let ZA be the derived Hamiltonian reduction ZA = T∗UA ×𝔩∗{0}. Then the derived critical locus of fA is defined via the following fibre product:

    Crit ⁡ [UA∕KA](fA) = [UA∕KA] ×[ZA∕KA]dfA,0[U A∕KA]

    Proposition 4.2. After possible further shrinking of open neighbourhoods around the reference connection A we have the following equivalence of open derived substacks:

    𝔐P CS(M) ≃ Crit ⁡ [U A∕KA] (fA).

    The idea of the proof is to exhibit the Chern–Simons stack as a Hamiltonian reduction, and compare the resulting charts with the ones constructed in Lemma 3.1.

    Proof

    Proof. We use the charts from Lemma 3.1 for the stack of connections [Conn ⁡ P (M)∕Gauge ⁡ P (M)]. Recall that at a connection A + a close to A the tangent space to the stack Conn ⁡ P (M) splits as V ⊕dA+aℓ and moreover, we have a splitting ΩA0 = 𝔩 ⊕ℓ. Using the map ιa−1 we normalize these splitting to be as follows:

    ιa−1d A+a = ( [a,−] 0 0 idℓ ) .

    What this tells us is that 𝔩 acts via infinitesimal symmetries [a,−] (also known as Noether maps), while ℓ acts by translation.

    Taking the V component, we obtain the moment map V ×V →(a,p)↦[a,−]∨p𝔩∗. We can cancel the ℓ factor throughout the fibre product definition of the Chern–Simons stack in terms of the connection stack. The result is given by:

    [V∕KA] ×[(V ×V )×𝔩∗{0}∕KA][V∕KA]

    Using the splitting of V as HA1 ×H∞im ⁡ dA∗ we can further project down to the HA1 component. This gives us the exact finite-dimensional model we wanted. Together with the identification of the projected equation with dfA = 0 we obtain the desired equivariant Darboux chart. Moreover, on the tangent spaces we obtain the following linearized model:

    [𝔩 →[ax,−]TxUA →Hess ⁡ xfATx∗U A →[ax,−]∨𝔩∗]
    □

    5 Proof of the main theorem

    To finish the proof of the main theorem, we need to show the properness of the diagonal and strictly speaking locally finite presentation, but this is more or less obvious from the construction of charts. To see properness we employ the following classical lemma, that tells us what the underlying topological space of 𝔐P 𝐶𝑆(M) is.

    Lemma 5.1. Pick a covering of the manifold M with (Mα,mα)’s. For a regularity parameter s ≥ 3 we have the following equivalence of quotient topological spaces induced by the holonomy map:

    Flat ⁡ s(P)∕Gauge ⁡ P s+1(M) →∼(∏ α=1NHom P|Mα(π1(Mα,mα);G))/GN.

    Moreover, the incidence locus Is = {(B0,B1,g)∣Rg(B0) = B1} evaluation at base points hα = g(mα)−1 is isomorphically identified with the closed locus inside of Flat ⁡ s(P)×2 ×GN cut out by finitely many equations of the form:

    hαHol ⁡ ̲B0(γ) = Hol ⁡ ̲B1(γ)hα,γ ∈Sα

    Here Sα is a choice of the generating set for π1(Mα,mα).

    Sometimes people call these γ’s Wilson loops, when thinking about more physicsy aspects of the Chern–Simons story.

    Everything about this lemma is standard except for the fact that one can do this at any Sobolev scale above 3 (this particular value is needed to make the holonomy map continuous). I will again leave the proof out for now; it is fairly straightforward and uses the standard (smooth) proof while keeping track of regularity.

    Proof of the main theorem

    Proof of the main theorem. As I have noted at the beginning of this section, it is enough to prove properness, as local finite presentation had already been established. We can use compactness of G and the above lemma to see that there exists a finite cover of 𝔐P 𝐶𝑆(M) with global quotients of the form Crit ⁡ [UAi∕KAi](fAi). Moreover, if we calculate the diagonal of the Čech groupoid for this covering, we get a derived stack that reduces to the incidence locus I. From the lemma, we again see that this is a compact space. Consequently the diagonal is proper. □

    6 Final remarks

    So what did we prove? Above, we have constructed a derived differential geometry refinement of the character variety for a closed 3-manifold.

    Moreover, by tracing through the constructions, it is not hard to see that it has the requisite shifted symplectic structure in the sense of Pantev–Toën–Vaquie–Vezzosi [PTVV13] (for the appropriate version of derived de Rham cohomology, see e.g. [Tar25]). More precisely, using the derived Hamiltonian reduction interpretation of the Chern–Simons stack, we have established the (−1)-shifted symplectic form arises in the standard way, as a loop for the contracting homotopies of the standard Liouville form on the derived critical locus.

    In formulas, this is the familiar symplectic form on the character variety:

    ωCS,A(α¯,β¯) = (−1)|α|∫ M⟨α ∧β⟩,α ∈ΩAp(M),β ∈Ω A3−p(M).

    This representability result is particularly nice because it provides a way to extract virtual invariants from the Chern–Simons stack, something I plan to address, hopefully, in a future post.

    7 Appendix: Functional analysis background

    Here I just wanted to briefly review the background from functional analysis and geometric PDEs required to make the above constructions rigorous. I will not go into the details of the proofs, but rather just give a brief overview of the relevant results, most of what I will say here is standard, I think that probably the best reference in the relevant geometric flavour is given in [Wel08, Chapter IV].

    7.1 Sobolev spaces lore

    The key point of the following definition is to use Banach models to work with something that is only Fréchet, i.e. we will present smooth sections of a vector bundle (this forms a Fréchet space) as an inverse limit of section spaces that are Banach.

    Let (M,g) be a closed Riemannian 3-manifold, let P →M be a principal bundle for a compact Lie group G, and fix an Ad ⁡ -invariant inner product on 𝔤. Choose a smooth reference connection A∗ on P. Combining the Levi–Civita connection with the connection induced by A∗ gives a covariant derivative

    ∇ ⁡A∗: Ωk(M;ad ⁡ P)→Ω1(M;ΛkT∗M ⊗ ad ⁡ P).

    For an integer r ≥ 0, define

    ∥α∥HA∗r2 = ∑ j=0r ∫ M |(∇ ⁡A∗)jα|2dvol ⁡ g,α ∈Ωk(M;ad ⁡ P).

    We write HrΩk(M;ad ⁡ P) for the completion of the smooth forms in this norm. Equivalently, if

    ΔA∗,k = dA∗dA∗∗ + d A∗∗d A∗

    is the covariant Hodge Laplacian on ad ⁡ P-valued k-forms, then an equivalent norm is

    ∥α∥HA∗r,sp = ∥(1 + ΔA∗,k)r∕2α∥ L2.

    Thus, if ΔA∗,kej = λjej and α = ∑ ⁡ jajej, then

    ∥α∥HA∗r,sp2 = ∑ j(1 + λj)r|a j|2.

    The spectral formula also defines Hr for arbitrary real r. On the compact manifold M, changing the smooth reference connection, metric, atlas, or bundle trivializations changes these norms only up to equivalence.

    The affine Hilbert space of Hs-connections is

    𝒜s(P) = A ∗ + HsΩ1(M;ad ⁡ P).

    For B = A∗ + a, its curvature is

    FB = FA∗ + dA∗a + 1 2[a ∧a] ∈Hs−1Ω2(M;ad ⁡ P),

    and we set

    Flat ⁡ s(P) = {B ∈𝒜s(P) : F B = 0}.

    The Sobolev gauge group is

    𝒢s+1(P) = Γ Hs+1(M;Ad ⁡ P),

    where Ad ⁡ P = P ×GG for the conjugation action of G on itself. This is a Hilbert Lie group modelled locally on Hs+1Ω0(M;ad ⁡ P); it is not itself a vector space with a canonical global norm. Equivalently, one may choose a faithful orthogonal representation G↪O(N) and use the ambient matrix-valued Sobolev topology.

    With the right-action convention, the gauge action is

    Bg −A ∗ = Ad ⁡ g−1(B −A∗) + g−1d A∗g.

    The shift from Hs-connections to Hs+1-gauge transformations is forced by the derivative in the second term.

    For integral s ≥ 3, Sobolev embedding in dimension three gives

    Hs(M)↪C1(M),Hs+1(M)↪C2(M),

    more precisely, into the corresponding Hölder spaces with any exponent below the endpoint. Moreover, Hs(M) is a Banach algebra. Consequently, curvature and the gauge action are smooth between the indicated Hilbert spaces, point evaluation g↦g(mα) is continuous, and holonomy along piecewise smooth loops depends continuously on an Hs-connection. In a local chart centered at a fixed smooth flat connection A, we take A∗ = A and abbreviate the resulting space by HrΩAk.

    7.2 Banach implicit function theorem

    We formulate the Banach implicit function theorem in its parametrized form, it serves as one of the main technical ingridients in our construction of local charts for 𝔐P 𝐶𝑆(M).

    Theorem 7.1. Let E,X,Y be real Banach spaces, P ⊆E and U ⊆X be open subsets and consider a Cr-continuous map:

    F : P ×U →Y.

    We consider the P to be the space of parameters, while U is the space of arguments for the map F. For concreteness assume that F(p0,x0) = 0 and assume that the Fréchet derivative in the U-direction is a bounded linear isomorphism.

    Then after potentially further shrinking open neighbourhoods P,U to P0,W0 and choosing a neighbourhood of the origin W ⊆Y there exists a unique Cr-map Ψ : P0 ×W0 →U0 such that Ψ Is the implicit function defined by F, i.e.

    F(p,Ψ(p,y)) = y, for all (p,y) ∈P0 ×W0.

    Definition 7.2. Suppose that 𝒱 ⊂L2(M;E) is a closed linear subspace and that U ⊂𝒱 ∩Hs(M;E). The constrained L2-gradient is the unique element

    ∇ ⁡L2𝒱 F(u) ∈𝒱

    satisfying

    DF(u)[v] = ⟨∇ ⁡L2𝒱 F(u),v⟩ L2,v ∈𝒱 ∩Hs(M;E).

    7.3 Elliptic lore

    The following is a standard piece of geometric PDEs lore; the key point of elliptic regularity is that it allows one to improve the regularity of solutions to elliptic partial differential equations.

    Definition 7.3. Let M be a smooth manifold, let E,F →M be smooth vector bundles, and let

    D: Γ∞(E)→Γ∞(F)

    be a linear differential operator of order m. In local coordinates and local trivializations of E and F, it can be written as

    D = ∑ |α|≤mAα(x)∂α,

    where Aα(x): Ex →Fx is a smooth family of linear maps. The principal symbol of D is the homogeneous degree-m bundle map

    σm(D): π∗E→π∗F

    over T∗M, defined by

    σm(D)(x,ξ) = ∑ |α|=mAα(x)ξα,(x,ξ) ∈T∗M.

    Some conventions replace ξα by (𝑖𝜉)α; this does not affect ellipticity. Although the displayed formula uses coordinates and trivializations, the resulting principal symbol is intrinsic.

    The operator D is called elliptic if

    σm(D)(x,ξ): Ex→Fx

    is an isomorphism for every x ∈M and every nonzero covector

    0≠ξ ∈Tx∗M.

    In particular, ellipticity forces rank ⁡ E = rank ⁡ F.

    If M is closed and D is elliptic of order m, then D extends to bounded operators

    D: Hs(M;E)→Hs−m(M;F)

    for every s ∈ ℝ, and satisfies elliptic estimates of the form

    ∥u∥Hs ≤Cs (∥𝐷𝑢∥Hs−m + ∥u∥Hs−1) .

    Consequently, D has finite-dimensional kernel and cokernel, closed image, and is Fredholm between the corresponding Sobolev spaces.

    Example 7.4. For a flat connection A the covariant Hodge Laplacian

    ΔA = dAdA∗ + d A∗d A

    has principal symbol

    σ2(ΔA)(x,ξ) = |ξ|g2 id ⁡ ,

    and is therefore elliptic. The operator dA alone is generally not elliptic as an operator, although the covariant de Rham complex may be an elliptic complex.

    Proposition 7.5 (elliptic estimate). Let M be a closed smooth manifold, let E,F →M be smooth vector bundles, and let

    D: Γ∞(E)→Γ∞(F)

    be an elliptic differential operator of order m. For every r ∈ ℝ, there is a constant Cr > 0 such that

    ∥u∥Hr+m ≤Cr (∥𝐷𝑢∥Hr + ∥u∥Hr+m−1)

    for every u ∈Hr+m(M;E). The lower-order term is necessary in general because D may have a nonzero finite-dimensional kernel.

    If E is equipped with a fiber metric and u is L2-orthogonal to ker⁡D, then the estimate can be strengthened to

    ∥u∥Hr+m ≤Cr∥𝐷𝑢∥Hr.

    In particular, this stronger estimate holds for every u if D is injective.

    Proposition 7.6 (elliptic regularity). if u ∈𝒟′(M;E) is a distributional section and

    𝐷𝑢 ∈Hr(M;F),

    then

    u ∈Hr+m(M;E).

    Thus an elliptic equation gains m derivatives. In particular,

    𝐷𝑢 ∈C∞(M;F)⟹u ∈C∞(M;E).

    More generally, elliptic regularity is local: for every open subset U ⊂M,

    𝐷𝑢 ∈Hlocr(U;F)⟹u ∈H locr+m(U;E).

    These statements follow from the existence of a pseudodifferential “almost inverse” callled a parametrix Q of order −m, satisfying

    𝑄𝐷 = id ⁡ −R,𝐷𝑄 = id ⁡ −S,

    where R and S are smoothing operators.
